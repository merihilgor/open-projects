# Low-Fee, Continuously Maintained Showers and Toilets at Every Public Beach

## Summary

Every beach with public beach status should be required to provide hygienic toilets and showers in numbers that match its capacity. Users would pay a small, symbolic fee. The fee discourages waste, and all of the money goes straight into keeping these facilities clean, staffed and maintained around the clock. The problem gets a permanent, sustainable fix that adds no burden to the public budget.

## Problem

- Many public beaches across the country have no adequate, working toilet and shower facilities. Where facilities do exist, poor hygiene and maintenance often leave them unusable.
- When people cannot meet basic needs at the beach, the result is serious pollution and a public health risk. In one example, people have relieved themselves into PET bottles and left them on the beach, which is shameful.
- This pollution harms the country's tourism image and public order.
- Experience shows that when these services are completely free, water and resource waste goes up, facilities are damaged within a short time, and cleaning cannot be kept up consistently.

## Proposed Solution

Make the following measures mandatory through legislation and administrative decisions:

1. **Mandatory infrastructure standards:** Every area with public beach status must install hygienic toilet and shower cabins in numbers suited to its capacity.
2. **Low-fee model to prevent waste:** These services are offered for a symbolic (small) fee, which prevents waste of resources and makes users more aware of how they use them.
3. **Sustainable maintenance and cleaning:** The symbolic revenue goes directly to funding round-the-clock (24/7) hygiene inspection of these facilities, permanent staff and periodic maintenance.

## How It Works

| Component | What it does | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Capacity-based infrastructure | Hygienic toilets and shower cabins sized to each public beach's capacity | Meets basic needs where people actually are, so there is no pollution on the beach |
| Symbolic usage fee | Users pay a small fee for each use | Prevents water and resource waste and builds a sense of responsible use |
| Earmarked revenue | Fee income is tied directly to the facilities it comes from | Funds 24/7 hygiene inspection, permanent staff and periodic maintenance without burdening the public budget |

The fee does not exist to make a profit. It is the tool that keeps the facilities working: it limits misuse and waste, and it pays for the cleaning and maintenance that free facilities have not been able to keep up.

## Implementation & Phasing

1. **Set standards:** Define national standards for public beach sanitation infrastructure, covering the number of toilets and showers relative to capacity and the hygiene criteria.
2. **Make it mandatory:** Turn these standards into requirements through legislation and administrative decisions.
3. **Instruct operators:** Direct the relevant municipalities and institutions responsible for public beaches to install and run the facilities under the low-fee model.
4. **Sustain operations:** Put fee revenue directly toward continuous hygiene inspection, staffing and periodic maintenance.

## Stakeholders & Benefits

**Implementing stakeholders**

- **Ministry of Environment, Urbanization and Climate Change (Türkiye):** responsible for the Coastal Law, the use of coastal areas, and environmental and public health standards.
- **Ministry of Culture and Tourism (Türkiye):** responsible for tourism standards and hygiene criteria at public beaches, and runs the "Free Public Beach" projects.
- **Municipalities (and relevant governorships):** directly responsible for the daily operation and infrastructure services of the beaches in their areas.

**Benefits**

- Protects public health.
- Prevents environmental pollution on beaches and coasts.
- Supports good-quality use of the coasts and a better tourism image and public order.
- Pays for itself through the fees, with no extra burden on the public budget.

## Risks & Mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Free facilities lead to water and resource waste | A symbolic usage fee discourages waste and encourages responsible use |
| Facilities get damaged within a short time | Fee revenue funds periodic maintenance and permanent staff |
| Cleaning cannot be kept up consistently | Revenue is earmarked for 24/7 hygiene inspection and continuous cleaning |
