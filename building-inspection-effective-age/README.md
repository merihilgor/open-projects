# Building Inspection and Effective Age Assessment System

Türkçe: [README.tr.md](README.tr.md)

## Summary

This proposal sets out a periodic **Building Inspection System** for the housing stock (houses, apartment buildings and residential complexes), modeled on vehicle inspection. Each building gets an **"Effective Age"** based on its physical condition, technical infrastructure, energy efficiency and safety standards, rather than only on its chronological age. The system aims to protect residents from unsafe and unhealthy conditions. It also aims to remove hidden costs and doubts when homes are bought or rented, to make housing valuation objective, to cut national energy use, to create jobs, and to make home insurance premiums easier to calculate and lower. It would be rolled out in phases so that older buildings are not burdened.

## Problem

- Homes are currently judged only by their chronological age. As a result, old buildings that are well maintained lose value. New buildings with poor technical infrastructure, such as rusty pipes or generators that do not work, carry hidden risks.
- When a home is sold or rented, buyers and tenants mostly judge it by how it looks. "Hidden defects" cause both economic loss and safety risk. Examples include rusty plumbing pipes, fire and generator systems that do not work, and low energy efficiency.
- Residents are exposed to unsafe and unhealthy conditions: rusty pipes, generators and alarms that do not work, detectors that do not work, and poorly maintained elevators.
- Inefficient lighting that has not been switched to LED bulbs and solar water heating systems (gün-ısı) that do not work or have lost efficiency push up energy use. This affects the country's total energy consumption.
- Purchase and rental processes carry hidden costs and doubts, and housing valuation has no objective basis.

## Proposed Solution

Set up a periodic, vehicle-inspection-style **Building Inspection and Effective Age Assessment System** for the housing stock, with three core components:

1. **Effective Age calculation.** Each building gets an "Effective Age" score based on its maintenance condition and technical infrastructure, rather than its chronological age. A 30-year-old building that is regularly maintained should count as more valuable and safer than a 10-year-old building that is neglected. Well-maintained buildings get a lower Effective Age.
2. **Technical inspection scope.** As with vehicle inspection, the following are inspected periodically: plumbing (rusty pipe checks), elevators, safety systems (alarm, fire, generator, hydrophore/booster pump), and energy efficiency (LED conversion of common-area lighting, efficiency of solar water heating systems).
3. **Energy and economic efficiency.** Inspections find inefficient lighting and heating systems, with the goal of lowering national energy use. Incentive mechanisms support this conversion.

## How It Works

### Inspection scope

| Area | Items inspected |
|---|---|
| Plumbing | Rusty pipe checks |
| Safety systems | Alarm, fire installation, detectors, generator, hydrophore |
| Vertical transport | Elevators |
| Energy efficiency | LED conversion of lighting (including common areas), efficiency of solar water heating systems, inefficient lighting and heating systems |

### Effective Age and the Building Inspection Report

- Each inspection produces a **Building Inspection Report** and an **inspection grade**, from which the building's **Effective Age** is calculated.
- The report becomes **mandatory in purchase, sale and rental transactions**. This prevents hidden costs and makes valuation objective and transparent.
- **Home insurance** risk premiums are set according to the building's inspection grade. Residents of well-maintained buildings get lower premiums.

### Inspection cycle

- As with vehicle inspection, buildings are inspected at regular intervals.
- New buildings are exempt for their first "n" years (proposed: 5 to 10 years). After that, periodic inspection becomes mandatory.

### Digital building record (QR code)

- Every building gets a **digital record** that holds its full history: inspection reports and grades, Effective Age, and the maintenance, repairs and renovations carried out.
- A **QR code** placed at the building entrance links to this record. Residents, prospective buyers, tenants, insurers and inspectors can see the building's current condition and history transparently.

### Incentive model: property tax discount

- Buildings that receive a **"full score"** in inspection get a **property tax discount**.
- Maintenance then pays off directly for owners and residents on top of the lower insurance premium, which encourages voluntary, regular maintenance.

## Implementation & Phasing

The system is rolled out gradually so that older buildings do not face sudden, heavy financial burdens:

| Phase | Scope |
|---|---|
| Phase 1 | New buildings: exemption for the first 5 to 10 years, followed by mandatory periodic inspection. |
| Phase 2 | Wider rollout, starting with public buildings and large residential complexes. |
| Phase 3 | Existing older buildings: "safety-first" inspections (elevator, fire, electrical), plus long-term credit facilities and incentive-based conversion models for energy-efficiency upgrades. |

**Implementing stakeholders:**

- **Ministry of Environment, Urbanization and Climate Change**: lead authority. The subject falls directly within its remit.
- **Ministry of Treasury and Finance**: coordination on the insurance component.
- **Ministry of Energy and Natural Resources**: coordination on the energy component.

## Stakeholders & Benefits

The system is designed so that **every stakeholder benefits** from it:

- **Residents:** less exposure to hazards such as rusty pipes, detectors that do not work and poorly maintained elevators, and a better quality of housing.
- **Buyers, sellers and tenants:** transparent market value, fewer hidden costs and doubts, and objective valuation.
- **Owners of well-maintained buildings:** a lower Effective Age, so the value of maintenance is recognized, plus a property tax discount for buildings with a full inspection score.
- **Insurers and policyholders:** risk premiums that are easier to calculate and lower for well-maintained buildings.
- **Workforce and industry:** a new line of business and new jobs for building inspection specialists, technical maintenance staff and renewable-energy installation teams.
- **The country:** energy savings in national consumption, a smaller foreign trade deficit through those savings, and a more sustainable urban fabric.

## Risks & Mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Heavy financial burden on older buildings | Phased rollout, a safety-first scope for old buildings, long-term credit facilities and incentive-based conversion models |
| Unnecessary burden on new buildings | Exemption for the first "n" years (5 to 10 years) |
| Cost of energy-efficiency upgrades | Incentive mechanisms for the conversion |

## References / Examples

Examples given in the original proposal:

- **Maintained vs. neglected:** a regularly maintained 30-year-old building should count as more valuable and safer than a neglected 10-year-old building.
- **Typical hidden defects:** rusty plumbing pipes, generators and alarms that do not work, detectors that do not work, and poorly maintained elevators.
- **Energy waste:** high-consumption lamps not replaced with LED bulbs, and solar water heating systems that do not work or have lost efficiency.
